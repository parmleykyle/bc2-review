Concrete floor color (dye at grind time)

Kyle decided on Oct 8: the slab is poured plain (no integral color), and all color goes on later, at grind time. Every option here is a dye applied after the pour. Integral color is ruled out. A natural, undyed grind isn't offered either, because on standard gray cement it reads gray. In plain words: (1) Dye vs. stain. All five options are dyes: thin, see-through color that soaks into the freshly ground surface. Prosoco calls its product a "stain", but it works like a dye. Acid stain (a chemical reaction that comes out blotchy and mottled) is not offered. (2) Aggregate exposure means how deep the grinder cuts, which decides how much stone shows. Class A "cream" stays at the surface: it looks smooth like plaster, but it needs a very flat slab and shows trowel marks. Class B "salt-and-pepper" shows fine sand specks and reads like limestone. Recommended. Class C "full aggregate" shows the big stones, which are usually gray gravel. (3) Sheen: Concrete Polishing Council Level 2, "satin/honed", a soft glow with no mirror reflections. Not Level 3 or 4, which are high gloss. Timing: dye needs no pour-time decision. Pick two finalists before the polishing sub's first grind, then approve one on a test patch ground on this slab before the full floor is dyed. Even with no color in the mix, the pour crew must know this slab will be polished: no curing compound or sealer that leaves a film, a flat finish suited to Class B, and the slab protected from oil, rust, drywall mud and markers during the build, because dye shows every mark. Chart colors are full strength over gray concrete, and the floor gets a lighter, diluted coat, so every screen color is an approximation. Sealer recommendation for Kyle's approval (it would fix; feature_log.csv has not been changed): Prosoco Consolideck LS densifier, then Consolideck PolishGuard, which is a guard made for polished concrete. Stop at satin, with no high-speed burnish to a gloss. Alternate: Ameripolish 3D SP. Skip Consolideck LSGuard, which is sold as a high-gloss guard. https://prosoco.com/product/polishguard/

Designer suggests: Light warm sand: Prosoco Consolideck GemTone Stain, Desert Sand (diluted). This is the lightest warm color that takes the gray out of the slab. It reads as pale limestone with the Creamy walls, light oak, brass and black iron, and it keeps the rugs and Van Courtland Blue as the color. Mocha is the safety net if the test patch leans olive. Finish: Class B salt-and-pepper, Level 2 satin, sealed with LS + PolishGuard.
